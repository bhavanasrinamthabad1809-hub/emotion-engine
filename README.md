You know how in Indian classical dance, dancers show emotions just with their face and body? In Kathak, those emotions are called the rasas, and the dancer’s footsteps keep the rhythm. We turned that into a small electronic instrument.

It’s a circuit board shaped like a lotus flower. Each petal has a big round button, and each button stands for one emotion: love, courage, sadness, joy or peace. When you press one, a little light behind it glows in that emotion’s color, and the light races across the petals like footsteps on a stage. That’s tatkar, the dancer’s stamping rhythm.

There’s also a knob on the board. Turn it, and the lights move faster or slower, like speeding up or slowing down the tempo of a dance (laya).

In the middle is a tiny amber light called the diya. In real performances, a lamp is lit before the dance begins, so ours flickers like a flame the whole time.

Tiny bells (ghungroo) hang from the petal tips, so the board jingles when you move it.

A small brain chip (an ESP32 board) reads the buttons and knob, and tells the lights what to do. If we have time, we’ll also make it play music notes.

So it’s art, culture and electronics in one thing: a board you feel with.
